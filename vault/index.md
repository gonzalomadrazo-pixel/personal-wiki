# Investing Wisdom Wiki

A personal study wiki built from my own notes on primary sources by five investors: Warren Buffett, Howard Marks, Charlie Munger, Nick Sleep and François Rochon. Start with a concept that interests you, follow its links to the sources that discuss it, and from any source page open the original note in `raw/`.

Folders: `raw/` holds the unchanged original notes (the evidence), `wiki/Sources` has one page per source, `wiki/Concepts` has one page per idea, and `wiki/Investors` has one page per author.

## Concepts by topic

### Risk

- [[Risk vs Volatility]]: Whether investment risk means price volatility (beta) or the chance of permanent loss. (3 sources)
- [[Margin of Safety]]: Paying a price low enough that the investment still works if things go wrong. (1 source)
- [[Leverage]]: Using borrowed money to magnify returns, and the fragility it creates. (1 source)
- [[Diversification vs Concentration]]: How many positions to hold, and when owning few or many makes sense. (3 sources)

### Psychology and Cycles

- [[Mr Market]]: Graham's allegory of an emotional market partner whose prices serve you but should not guide you. (2 sources)
- [[Market Cycles]]: The recurring, self-correcting swings of economies, companies and investor psychology. (3 sources)
- [[Credit Cycle]]: How lending standards loosen in good times and tighten in bad, amplifying booms and busts. (1 source)
- [[Contrarian Investing]]: Buying what others are avoiding, because only unpopular assets can be truly cheap. (2 sources)
- [[Limits of Forecasting]]: Why macro and market forecasts rarely add value, and how to invest without them. (2 sources)

### Business Quality and Value

- [[Price and Value]]: The relationship between what you pay and what a business is worth. (5 sources)
- [[Competitive Moats]]: Durable advantages that protect a business's returns from competitors. (4 sources)
- [[Scale Economies Shared]]: Nomad's model: businesses that pass scale savings back to customers to grow a stronger moat. (1 source)
- [[Owner Earnings]]: Judging investments by the underlying earnings of the businesses owned rather than by stock quotes. (3 sources)
- [[Capital Allocation]]: How managers deploy the cash a business generates, and why it matters to owners. (1 source)

### Process and Temperament

- [[Circle of Competence]]: Investing only in what you can genuinely understand, and knowing where that boundary lies. (7 sources)
- [[Patience and Long Holding Periods]]: Why waiting, and holding good businesses for years, is a source of return. (4 sources)
- [[Rule of Three]]: Giverny's empirical rule about how often markets fall, stocks disappoint and a portfolio lags. (1 source)
- [[Learning From Mistakes]]: Reviewing past decisions honestly, including sales and missed purchases. (2 sources)
- [[Mental Models]]: Thinking with many models from different disciplines instead of one formula. (2 sources)
- [[Opportunity Cost]]: Judging every investment against your best available alternative. (1 source)
- [[Aligned Incentives]]: Structuring fees, ownership and pay so managers and investors win or lose together. (2 sources)

## Sources by investor

### [[Warren Buffett]]

- [[Buffett 1987 Letter]]: This study note summarizes Warren Buffett's 1987 letter, focusing on his investment philosophy regarding business value, market sentiment, and capital allocation.
- [[Buffett 1993 Letter]]: This study note presents Warren Buffett's arguments against using volatility as the primary measure of investment risk, advocating instead for a focus on the long-term economics of businesses.
- [[Buffett 1996 Letter]]: This study note summarizes Warren Buffett's 1996 letter, focusing on investment principles such as inactivity, selecting businesses within one's circle of competence, and avoiding past mistakes.

### [[Howard Marks]]

- [[Marks 2001 Cycles Memo]]: Howard Marks argues that while economic forecasts are unreliable, understanding the inevitable cycles and knowing one's current position within them is crucial for preparation.
- [[Marks 2003 Most Important Thing]]: This study note outlines Howard Marks' core investment principles, emphasizing the importance of price and value, defensive investing, and avoiding reliance on macro forecasts.
- [[Marks 2006 Risk Memo]]: This memo by Howard Marks focuses on redefining risk, arguing that volatility is not the same as risk and that true risk involves the likelihood of permanent loss or unacceptably low returns.

### [[Charlie Munger]]

- [[Munger 2016 Daily Journal Meeting]]: This study note covers the investing parts of Charlie Munger's 2016 Daily Journal Q&A.

### [[Nick Sleep]]

- [[Nomad 2006 Annual Letter]]: This note details Nick Sleep's 2006 annual letter from Nomad Investment Partnership, arguing that patience in holding good businesses and understanding the long-term value of scale economies are key to investment success.

### [[Francois Rochon]]

- [[Giverny 2002 Annual Letter]]: This study note summarizes François Rochon's 2002 annual letter to Giverny Capital clients, focusing on market realities, investment philosophy, and the importance of long-term perspective.
