# Competitive Moats

*Topic group: Business Quality and Value.* Durable advantages that protect a business's returns from competitors.

## What the sources say

- **[[Buffett 1993 Letter]]** (Warren Buffett): Companies like Coke and Gillette possess protective moats through their brands, products, and distribution, which provide long-term business risk protection, even if their betas look like ordinary companies.
- **[[Buffett 1996 Letter]]** (Warren Buffett): Buffett calls Coca-Cola and Gillette 'The Inevitables', companies almost certain to dominate their fields worldwide for an investment lifetime, and warns that most apparent leaders are 'Impostors' vulnerable to competitive attack.
- **[[Munger 2016 Daily Journal Meeting]]** (Charlie Munger): Munger says a software business that becomes entrenched can be very 'sticky', while autos are brutally competitive and commoditised, so an investor there would need a clearly superior competitor.
- **[[Nomad 2006 Annual Letter]]** (Nick Sleep): An earlier Nomad appendix proposed measuring a moat's strength by how a firm splits scale benefits between customers, employees and shareholders (the 'robustness ratio'), using GEICO as the example.

## Related concepts

- [[Scale Economies Shared]]: sharing scale savings with customers is one way a moat widens
- [[Circle of Competence]]: you can only judge a moat in a business you understand
