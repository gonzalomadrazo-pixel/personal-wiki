# Price and Value

*Topic group: Business Quality and Value.* The relationship between what you pay and what a business is worth.

## What the sources say

- **[[Buffett 1987 Letter]]** (Warren Buffett): Buffett stresses that what matters is growth in per-share business value, not book value, and he prefers a wonderful business at a sensible price over a mediocre business at a bargain price.
- **[[Marks 2001 Cycles Memo]]** (Howard Marks): Marks notes that asset prices often move far more than cash flows because investor psychology changes valuations, and he trims cyclical companies when the market forgets that peak earnings deserve trough valuations.
- **[[Marks 2003 Most Important Thing]]** (Howard Marks): Marks says the relationship between price and value comes first: a good company is not the same as a good investment, and the biggest losses come from buying 'perfect' companies at prices that assume perfection.
- **[[Marks 2006 Risk Memo]]** (Howard Marks): Marks says risky assets can be good investments if they are cheap enough, and the skill lies in knowing when that is the case.
- **[[Munger 2016 Daily Journal Meeting]]** (Charlie Munger): Munger says fundamental value investing will never go out of style because success requires buying things for less than they are worth; Bank of America was bought after it fell far below its value.

## Related concepts

- [[Margin of Safety]]: a wide gap between price and value is the margin of safety
- [[Owner Earnings]]: value comes from the earnings a business produces for its owners
