# Marks 2001 Cycles Memo

**Author:** [[Howard Marks]] · **Date:** 2001-11-20 · **Original note:** [[raw/marks-2001-cycles-memo|marks-2001-cycles-memo.md]] · **Original document:** the-complete-collection.pdf (PDF pp. 135-145)

## Summary

Howard Marks argues that while economic forecasts are unreliable, understanding the inevitable cycles and knowing one's current position within them is crucial for preparation. He emphasizes that markets move based on investor psychology and that cycles correct themselves, suggesting investors should focus on positioning rather than trying to predict the timing of future moves.

## Key points

- Economic forecasts are generally not valuable because markets already reflect the consensus view, and non-consensus views are rarely correct. *(note section: The economic cycle)*
- Cycles are inevitable and self-correcting; success and failure create the conditions for the next cycle. *(note section: General rules about cycles)*
- The credit cycle involves how lending standards loosen during prosperity and tighten during downturns, which amplifies economic swings. *(note section: The credit cycle)*
- Companies move through a life cycle involving start-up, growth, maturity, and decline, with bankruptcy potentially restarting the cycle. *(note section: The corporate life cycle, and business fads)*
- Asset prices are driven more by investor psychology and valuation changes than by cash flows. *(note section: The market cycle and the pendulum)*
- Investors should buy cyclical companies when the economy is depressed and trim when demand is booming and the market overvalues peak earnings. *(note section: The economic cycle)*

## Concepts in this source

- [[Market Cycles]]: Marks believes cycles are inevitable and that investors should focus on knowing where they are in a cycle rather than trying to predict its timing.
- [[Credit Cycle]]: The credit cycle describes how changes in the economy cause swings in credit availability, where loosening standards during upswings and tightening during downswings amplify asset price movements.
- [[Price and Value]]: Marks notes that asset prices often move far more than cash flows because investor psychology changes valuations, and he trims cyclical companies when the market forgets that peak earnings deserve trough valuations.
- [[Limits of Forecasting]]: Marks dismisses economic forecasts as adding little value, stating that markets already incorporate the consensus forecast.
- [[Contrarian Investing]]: Marks copes with cycles by leaning against the wind: investing in cyclical companies when the economy is depressed and observers are gloomy, and preferring arenas where others are not throwing money.
